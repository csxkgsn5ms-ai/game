# Prompt for Claude: Should a sales funnel help sell my surplus plumbing fixtures?

Copy everything below the line into a new Claude conversation that has web search turned on.

---

You are a research analyst. I need a practical, evidence-based recommendation, not a pep talk.

## My situation

- Business: **Luxury Plumbing Surplus** (luxuryplumbingsurplus.com), a small local seller in **Gilbert, Arizona**.
- Inventory: about **118 new, unused** kitchen and bath fixtures from premium brands: Rohl, Brizo, Riobel, Phylrich, Newport Brass, Jaclo, Isenberg/Flusso, Moen, Delta, Kohler, Robern, MTI, QMdrain and others. Items include faucets, shower trims, rough valves, linear drains, towel bars, medicine cabinets and a sink.
- Pricing: about **50% of manufacturer retail** for fixtures and 30% for cartridges and small parts. Asking prices run from **$5 to $1,455**. Most items are $100 to $400.
- Quantity: usually 1 or 2 of each model, so every SKU is close to one-of-a-kind.
- Current channels: a simple catalog website and **75 live Facebook Marketplace listings**. Pickup in Gilbert by appointment; shipping on some items.
- Problem: the Marketplace listings are not selling and the photos are weak. I am rebuilding the listings now. Separately, I want to know whether a **funnel** would move this inventory faster.
- Constraints: I am one person with a phone camera, a small budget, and limited time. I want to sell out this inventory, not build a long-term media brand unless it clearly pays off.

## What to research

1. **Could a funnel help at all?** Look at these options and any others you find credible:
   - Paid ads (Facebook/Instagram ads, Marketplace boosted listings, Google Shopping or Search)
   - Instagram posts and Reels (before/after, "fixture of the day", finish close-ups)
   - A long-form YouTube video (for example, a walkthrough of the whole fixture room)
   - Short-form video (Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts)
   - Contractor, designer, remodeler and flipper outreach (local trade groups, Nextdoor, supply-house bulletin boards, Houzz, Facebook groups)
   - Other resale channels: eBay, OfferUp, Craigslist, Habitat ReStore consignment, liquidation buyers, bulk lot sale
   - A simple funnel: ad or post → website catalog → Messenger or text → pickup appointment
2. **Find real examples.** Look for people or businesses who sold surplus, overstock, salvage or discontinued plumbing fixtures, building materials, or similar high-ticket home-improvement items through these channels. Good places to look: surplus building-material stores, architectural salvage shops, reseller YouTube channels and case studies, Reddit (r/Flipping, r/smallbusiness, r/Plumbing, r/HomeImprovement), and marketing case studies. For each example, record what they sold, the channel, what they did, and any reported result (numbers if available).
3. **Compare what worked.** Build a comparison table with these columns: channel, cost to start, weekly time required, how fast it typically produces sales, which buyers it reaches (DIY homeowner, contractor, designer, flipper), fit for 1–2 unit SKUs at $100–$1,500, and strength of evidence.
4. **Recommend.** Give me the single most practical plan for my situation, plus a 30-day action plan with weekly steps, a budget, and what to measure so I know within 2 to 3 weeks whether it's working. Say plainly if a funnel is **not** worth it and simpler steps (better listings, a bulk-lot offer, contractor outreach) are the better bet.

## Look up your Claude skills

Before you start, check which Claude skills and tools you have access to in this conversation (for example, by listing your available skills). Tell me which ones could help with:
- the research itself (web search, data extraction)
- writing listing copy, ad copy, captions and video scripts (a marketing or copywriting skill, or a skill that removes AI-sounding phrasing)
- images (a design or canvas skill for a simple branded cover image or price-sheet graphic; note that edits to product photos must never misrepresent the item)
- video (a video-creation skill such as Remotion, for simple product slideshow videos)
- building the funnel (a landing page, a web artifact, or a spreadsheet tracker)

For each skill you name, say what you would use it for and whether you actually have it. Don't claim skills you can't confirm.

## Rules for your answer

- **Separate evidence from guesses.** Tag every important claim as **[Evidence]** (backed by a source you link), **[Expert opinion]** (a practitioner's stated view, linked), or **[My estimate]** (your reasoning, with no source).
- **Link every source** inline, and end with a source list. Prefer sources from the last 2 years. If you can't find evidence for something, say so rather than filling the gap.
- Don't invent case studies, numbers or quotes. If an example has no reported result, say "no result reported."
- Be specific to used and surplus home-improvement goods sold locally, not generic e-commerce advice.
- Keep it practical: put the recommendation and 30-day plan first, then the comparison table, then the examples and sources.
